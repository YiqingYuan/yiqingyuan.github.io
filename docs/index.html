Independent Research

Government Procurement Law and Firm Innovation

Funded by Fudan University’s Undergraduate Research Opportunity Program (UROP), Second Prize in Fudan University’s Academic Innovation Challenge

This paper examines the impact of the 2003 Chinese Government Procurement Law (GPL) on firms’ technological innovation, using a DID design. The GPL is the first nation-wide regulation of government procurement, which mandated open bidding as the principal form, promoting an open and competitive procurement market. The results indicate that the GPL significantly increased firms’ patent applications and R&D investment in procurement-intensive industries. The effects are greater for more efficient firms, and in more corrupt cities. This pattern is consistent with the objective of the GPL, which was to promote an open procurement market and reallocate government contracts toward more efficient firms, particularly in places that were previously more corrupt and distorted. By doing so, the GPL has strengthened firms’ incentives to invest in R&D, and fostered innovation growth. I further propose a research design to investigate the underlying mechanisms of how obtaining government contracts affects subsequent innovation, using a novel contract dataset

Research assistant to Professor Jacob Moscona (MIT)

Archival Data on China’s Fuel and Chemical Industry, 1962–1995

Research assistant to Professor Ting Chen (Hong Kong Baptist University)

I built a novel firm-level panel dataset from the Chinese Fuel and Chemical Industry yearbooks (1962–1995). I designed and implemented a data-cleaning pipeline that combines locally hosted LLM parsing, rule-based standardisation, and edit-distance–based matching to harmonise variables and firm names across years. I then linked yearbook firms to the 1985 and 1995 Industrial Census data to enrich the panel with additional firm characteristics. This archival dataset will be used to study the economic effects of China’s reform and opening-up.

Environmental Regulation and Firms’ Low-Carbon Transition

Research assistant to Professor Xueyue Liu (Fudan University)

I assisted with a study on environmental regulation and firms’ low-carbon transition. My tasks included translating and proofreading the final manuscript and using Stata for data cleaning, analysis, and visualization based on the U.S. Occupational Information Network (O*NET) database. This work deepened my experience working with large, detailed occupational-task data and empirical analysis in environmental economics.
